# CASp Test Materials — Fact and Coverage Database

## Purpose and evidence rules

This database consolidates the 64 selected conversations in `Selected Chats/selected_chats_flat.json` and their summary in `Selected Chats/chat_summaries.md`. It is intended to organize covered material and identify what can become test questions; it is **not** a substitute for the controlling code, standard, statute, or official exam guide.

The flat export includes branched conversations, repeated answers, and assistant-generated claims. A claim appearing in a chat is evidence that the topic was discussed, not proof that the claim is correct. In particular, earlier answers sometimes contradicted the user's corrections. Statuses below make that distinction explicit:

- **Captured topic** — the conversation establishes that this subject was studied, but not necessarily its answer.
- **User-recalled; verify** — based on the user's study notes or exam recollection; confirm against primary material.
- **Assistant claim; verify** — appeared in a previous assistant answer and must not be used as a keyed answer without checking.
- **Conflicted** — inconsistent claims appeared in the chats; do not use as an answer until resolved.
- **Open question** — the conversation raised the question but did not establish a dependable answer.
- **Process guidance** — a suggested study or inspection method, not a code requirement.

For a production question bank, store the authority, edition/effective date, exact section, and verification date alongside every answer. Do not silently carry a requirement across code editions or jurisdictions.

## Major categories and study approach

| Category | Coverage in the chats | Recommended test treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Exam orientation and learning strategy | Study plans, one-at-a-time hard questions, true/false practice, test-taking and post-exam debrief | Use timed scenarios and retrieval practice; separate official exam facts from candidate recollection. |
| 2. Code framework, definitions, and applicability | CBC Chapters 11A/11B, code divisions, definitions, agency/adoption matrix, ADA, FHA, UFAS, and California law | Ask the learner to identify the governing authority and edition before selecting a dimensional answer. |
| 3. Scoping and alteration triggers | CBC 11B-404.3, project triggers, path-of-travel questions, percentage comparisons, and funding/jurisdiction questions | Use a decision-tree format: facility/use, project scope, alteration/new work, applicable code, then technical criteria. |
| 4. Accessible routes and site circulation | Routes between parking, entrances, buildings, and campus destinations; route continuity and vehicle conflicts | Use plans and field scenarios; distinguish a route requirement from the question of which work is in project scope. |
| 5. Parking and EV charging | Accessible car/van spaces, aisles, markings, signs, vertical clearance, counts, EV charging stations | Test accessible parking and EV charging as related but distinct scopes. Keep every EV-specific claim blocked pending code verification. |
| 6. Ramps, doors, elevators, and general elements | Ramp details, door clearances/openings, elevator accessibility and lobbies, and related building elements | Prefer diagrams and measurement scenarios; distinguish rough opening from required clear opening. |
| 7. Plumbing and toilet facilities | Division 6 plumbing elements, accessible toilet/urinal/lavatory topics, fixture calculations, and 11A/11B comparison | Separate plumbing fixture-count calculations from accessibility scoping; do not use the unresolved ratios in the chats. |
| 8. Transient lodging | Occupancy classification, public areas, guestrooms, room types, dispersion, routes, and reservation information | Combine classification and accessibility-scoping questions; check each answer against the applicable lodging provisions. |
| 9. Special occupancies and facilities | Recreation facilities, transportation facilities, public hospitals, and all-gender restrooms | Organize by facility type and applicable code section; don't generalize one facility's exception to another. |
| 10. CASp inspection and reporting | Report structure, survey work, project-phase review, field observations, and risk-oriented thinking | Practice documenting a finding with location, observation, measurement, applicable criterion, and disposition; label legal-risk analysis separately from code compliance. |
| 11. Fair housing and other legal standards | California FHA history, FHA outside California, ADA/UFAS distinctions, and private multifamily questions | Use comparison matrices. Do not infer that one standard displaces another without project-specific authority. |
| 12. Adjacent building and life-safety topics | Egress widths, emergency lighting, I-4 analysis, and code-cycle updates/errata | Keep these in a cross-disciplinary module. Ask which discipline and adopted code control before treating them as CASp accessibility facts. |

### Blueprint-driven study and item-writing rules

1. **Weight the closed-book bank to the published distribution.** For every 60-question full-length set, allocate 24 CBC/ADAAG public-accommodation and commercial-facility items, 12 housing items, 8 federal-accessibility items, 8 CASp/California statutory items, and 8 standards-identification scenarios.
2. **Keep the two standards-selection tasks distinct.** The 24-question category asks about CBC/ADAAG scoping and general/technical application in public accommodations/commercial facilities. The separate 8-question category tests choosing one or more applicable standards from scenario facts.
3. **Build an explicit housing comparison matrix.** Cover the named federal and state sources and distinguish private housing (2025 CBC Chapter 11A) from public housing (2025 CBC Chapter 11B), using the terminology and scope in the official exam materials and code.
4. **Add California law and CASp-program coverage.** The prior chat-derived fact set was weighted toward building-code dimensions; it needs a dedicated 8-question batch on CASp responsibilities/program regulations and disability-access statutes across the Civil Code, Government Code, and Health and Safety Code.
5. **Treat item counts as verified from the supplied excerpt, not other exam logistics.** The excerpt establishes the closed-book content areas and their counts, but does not establish the time limit, passing score, question format, or pass rate.
6. **Require provenance for keyed answers.** Every scored question should cite its controlling statute, regulation, standard, code section/table, edition, and effective date. For “Identifying Applicable Standards” items, explanations should state which scenario facts activate or exclude each standard.
